06

Application Experience

The one section where sentiment turns net negative. This is a build/UX risk, not an instructional one — worth separating cleanly from §§04–05 when this report is read.

Single largest actionable finding in this report

At least 12 of 33 responses, independently, across two separate free-text questions, named the same specific problem: low-contrast text (gray-on-gray, gray-on-black), animated scanline/CRT-style visual effects, and motion graphics that were hard to read and — for several students — correlated with their laptop crashing or needing "graphics acceleration" disabled in the browser to stay usable. This was volunteered, not prompted by a leading question.

Underneath the visual-effects complaint, the application otherwise reads as well-liked in concept: 22 of 33 free-text answers on "what did you like most" praised the format itself — link analysis, squad standings, role-tailored views, the drop-based structure — unprompted, in the same survey. This is a legibility/performance-tuning problem on a concept respondents want kept, not a request to abandon the format.

07

Data Notes & Methodology

  • 36 → 33 roster cleaning. Three accounts were excluded from every figure in this report: one system-flagged duplicate (inactive, duplicate+…invalid email), one unused duplicate signup that never logged in (a second, real account for the same student has 23 submissions and is included), and one account matching this course's known staff-preview-account pattern. All three carried squad assignments and would otherwise have skewed squad rosters and course-wide averages.
  • Grade distribution and average are recomputed from the clean roster, not read directly from the platform's course-analytics summary, which still includes the 3 excluded accounts.
  • The platform's built-in "at-risk" metric was deliberately not used in this report. Its submission-rate calculation divides each student's submitted-assignment count by the course-wide total of published assignments (167) rather than the subset actually visible to that student under role-based content gating — so it currently flags effectively the entire cohort as "at risk" regardless of actual performance. That is a platform bug, not a cohort finding; it is noted here so the number doesn't surface elsewhere without this context.
  • Pre/post scores are matched by student account via the platform's pretest/posttest assignment records. 2 of 33 students are missing one side of the pair and are excluded only from the pre/post aggregate (§03), not from participation or survey figures.
  • Survey results are anonymous by design and cannot be — and were not — matched back to individual students; §04–§06 report cohort-level aggregates and unattributed verbatim quotes only.
